How it works in 30 seconds

  1. One permission. Touchgrass uses Android's Accessibility service for one thing: noticing when you open a short-form feed inside an app you put on the block list.
  2. Detect the feed. When it sees one, it presses Back. You're out before the algorithm gets a hold of you.
  3. Stay out. A debounce stops spam-tap re-entries. A watchdog makes sure the service stays alive across reboots, OEM background-killers, and updates.

Questions people ask

Does Touchgrass read my messages?
No. The Accessibility service is scoped strictly to the apps you put on the block list. Even within those, Touchgrass only reads UI elements long enough to detect a feed. You can audit the configuration in the source code.
Does anything leave my phone?
Only one thing, and only if you turn it on: the commitment lock sends a one-time code to your own email via a transactional-email service. Your email address is stored on your phone only. There's no Touchgrass server that knows you exist.
Why Android only?
iOS doesn't have an equivalent of Android's Accessibility API for this purpose. iOS Screen Time + Family Controls can do some of it. An iOS V2 is on the roadmap.
Is it really free?
Yes. No "premium" tier at launch. No tier-locked features. If something changes later, anyone who installed during the free era keeps everything free.
What happens if Instagram changes its UI?
The detector breaks until we ship an update. Because Touchgrass is open source, you can also fix it yourself — see the contributing guide on GitHub.
iOS user? We're planning an iOS version (V2). It will use Apple's Family Controls + a browser extension. Join the list and we'll tell you when it's ready.
